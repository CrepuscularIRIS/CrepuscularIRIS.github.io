# One Polyp Identifies All

## One-Line Summary

One-shot polyp segmentation via SAM with cascaded priors and iterative prompt evolution — a practical medical imaging advance.

## Why This Is Worth Your Time

Most medical segmentation models demand **hundreds of annotated examples** per lesion type. Colonoscopy datasets are small, polyp morphology varies wildly, and annotating endoscopic video is expensive. Mao et al. sidestep this by treating SAM as a few-shot learner: **one annotated polyp** becomes the prior that bootstraps segmentation across the entire case. This is not a theoretical curiosity — it is a direct answer to the annotation bottleneck that stalls clinical deployment of vision foundation models.

The second angle is architectural pragmatism. Instead of fine-tuning SAM's massive weights on a handful of endoscopy frames, the authors keep SAM frozen and manipulate its **prompt space**. Cascaded priors stack spatial and semantic cues; iterative prompt evolution refines them frame by frame. The model learns to ask better questions, not to rewrite its internal answers.

## Core Method (3-Minute Version)

SAM segments by responding to **prompts** — points, boxes, or masks. In one-shot polyp segmentation, the challenge is that a single example polyp must generalise to new polyps with different shapes, sizes, and lighting conditions.

Mao et al. build a **cascaded prior pipeline**:

1. **Spatial prior** — the one-shot mask anchors a rough region of interest.
2. **Semantic prior** — features from the example polyp guide SAM's encoder toward polyp-like textures in the target frame.
3. **Iterative prompt evolution** — the output mask from step *t* becomes the prompt for step *t+1*, refined by a lightweight correction network that learns to nudge SAM when it under-segments or over-segments.

The key insight is **composability**. Each prior is weak alone; chained together they create a feedback loop that tightens boundaries without ever updating SAM's frozen parameters. This keeps the method fast and keeps it from overfitting to tiny datasets.

## Limits and Unanswered Questions

- **No concrete limitations in source.** Anticipated concerns, pending the full paper:
  - How does the method behave on **sessile serrated adenomas**, which are flat and visually subtle? The one-shot prior may not capture enough texture variation.
  - **Temporal drift** in long videos: iterative evolution assumes frame-to-frame similarity. Sudden camera withdrawal or bowel prep artefacts could break the chain.
  - The correction network is small, but it still needs **some training data**. The authors claim one-shot; the exact boundary between "zero" and "one" shot here deserves scrutiny.
